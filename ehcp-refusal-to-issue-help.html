Assessed, but refused an EHCP?

After assessment, the local authority may decide that an EHCP is not necessary. This decision needs a different evidence review from a refusal to assess. Compare the assessment advice, recommended provision and support available without a plan, then consider free information or separately agreed help with your next step.

Confirm the decision stage

A refusal to issue happens after an EHC needs assessment. The local authority (LA) has considered the assessment evidence but decided not to make a plan. An assessment does not guarantee an EHCP. If the LA has not assessed your child at all, start with refusal-to-assess support.

A refusal to issue must normally be notified within 16 weeks of the original assessment request, subject to statutory exceptions. If a plan is issued, the normal final-plan deadline is 20 weeks. IPSEA explains these process timings; they are not predictions of how long an appeal will take.

Is a plan necessary?

At this stage, section 37(1) of the Children and Families Act 2014 applies. The question is whether it is necessary for special educational provision to be made in accordance with an EHC plan. The earlier assessment-stage “may be necessary” test is not the final issue-stage test.

Consider all the assessment advice together and whether your child will receive the special educational provision they need without a plan. A diagnosis alone does not settle that question. Nor does a general statement that the school has a SEN budget. The IPSEA issuance guidance explains why assessed needs must be considered alongside provision.

Assessment advice versus available provision

EHCP refusal to issue help starts with needs-to-provision evidence. Gather the LA decision letter, advice obtained during assessment and current school support plan. For each relevant need, identify the recommended provision, what the school says is available and evidence of what is actually delivered.

Record gaps and disagreements accurately. Keep a professional's recommendation distinct from your own observations or questions; do not attribute a recommendation they did not make.

Fictional example: a needs-to-provision check

This is a planning illustration, not a prediction about an appeal.

Need identified
The assessment describes a communication need affecting participation in lessons.
Provision recommended
A report sets out a structured communication programme with specialist oversight.
School position
The school says support can be provided from existing arrangements.
Evidence question
Who will deliver the programme, how will specialist oversight be arranged, and what shows that the recommended support is available and sufficient?

A clear comparison may identify missing advice, uncertainty about provision or a disagreement about whether a plan is necessary. Sometimes clarifying delivery arrangements with school is more useful than immediately commissioning another report.

Use the refusal-to-issue appeal route

For a refusal after assessment, the form for an appeal by post or email is normally SEND35, except where a different custody form applies. An online route may be available. Follow the current official submission instructions, including the decision letter, mediation certificate, reasons and relevant evidence. Keep copies and follow the tribunal's directions.

If the LA has issued an EHCP but you disagree with its wording or named school, that is not a refusal-to-issue appeal. If an existing plan specifies provision but it is not delivered, the responsibility and remedy are different again. See help with school SEN support, plan wording and missing provision and ask which route matches your situation.

Check appeal deadlines while you decide

The tribunal must normally receive your appeal within two calendar months from the date of the LA decision letter or one month from the date of the mediation certificate, whichever is later. See the official deadline and submission guidance.

For a refusal appeal, you normally need to contact a mediation adviser, consider mediation and obtain a certificate. Attending mediation is optional. If you decline it, contact the mediation service for the certificate, as GOV.UK explains. IPSEA's certificate guidance explains the requirement to contact the adviser within two months of the decision letter.

A conversation with the LA, a school complaint or booking a consultation does not pause the appeal deadline. If it is close, follow the official instructions and seek prompt advice. If you are late, ask about permission for a late appeal; acceptance is not automatic.

Questions parents ask

Does completing an assessment mean the LA must issue an EHCP?

Not automatically. The issue-stage decision turns on whether it is necessary for special educational provision to be made in accordance with an EHC plan.

What if the school says it can provide everything?

Compare that statement with the assessment recommendations, delivery arrangements and available evidence. Ask for a clear account of how identified needs will be met, rather than assuming either that a plan is unnecessary or that support cannot be provided.
