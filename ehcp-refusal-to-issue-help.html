Assessed, but refused an EHCP?

Your child has been through the assessment, but the LA says a plan is not necessary. We help you compare the assessed needs, the recommended provision and what can actually be secured without an EHCP.

This is a different decision from refusing to assess

A refusal to issue happens after an EHC needs assessment. The LA has considered the assessment evidence but decided not to make a plan. An assessment does not automatically produce an EHCP. If the LA has not assessed your child at all, start with refusal-to-assess support.

A refusal to issue must normally be notified within 16 weeks of the original assessment request, subject to statutory exceptions. If a plan is issued, the normal final-plan deadline is 20 weeks. Those are assessment-process deadlines, not predictions of how long an appeal will take.

The question is whether a plan is necessary

At this stage, section 37(1) of the Children and Families Act 2014 applies. The question is whether it is necessary for special educational provision to be made in accordance with an EHC plan. The earlier assessment-stage “may be necessary” test is not the final issue-stage test.

Focus on the provision the evidence identifies and whether your child will receive the special educational provision they need without a plan. A diagnosis alone does not settle that question. Nor does a general statement that the school has a SEN budget.

Compare needs, recommendations and actual support

Start with the LA decision letter, the advice gathered during assessment and the current school support plan. For each relevant need, identify the recommended provision, what the school says is available and the evidence of what is actually delivered. Record gaps and disagreements accurately; do not rewrite a professional's recommendation as something they did not say.

Fictional example: a needs-to-provision check

This is a planning illustration, not a prediction about an appeal.

Need identified
The assessment describes a communication need affecting participation in lessons.
Provision recommended
A report sets out a structured communication programme with specialist oversight.
School position
The school says support can be provided from existing arrangements.
Evidence question
Who will deliver the programme, how will specialist oversight be arranged, and what shows that the recommended support is available and sufficient?

A clear comparison can reveal missing assessment advice, uncertainty about provision or a genuine disagreement about whether a plan is necessary. It can also show that a further question to the school would be more useful than immediately commissioning another report.

Use the refusal-to-issue appeal route

The relevant form is normally SEND35 for a child or young person living in the community. Include the LA decision letter, mediation certificate and your reasons and relevant evidence, following the official submission instructions. Keep copies and follow any directions issued by the tribunal.

If the LA has issued an EHCP but you disagree with its wording or named school, that is not a refusal-to-issue appeal. If an existing plan specifies provision but it is not delivered, the responsibility and remedy are different again. See help when school SEN support is failing and ask which route matches your situation.

Protect the appeal deadline while you decide

For these refusal appeals, the tribunal must normally receive your appeal by the later of two calendar months from the LA decision letter or one month from the mediation certificate. You normally need to contact the mediation adviser within two months, consider mediation and obtain a certificate. You do not have to attend mediation. The decision letter should explain how to contact the relevant adviser.

A conversation with the LA, a school complaint or booking a consultation does not pause the appeal deadline. If the deadline is close, follow the current official submission instructions and seek prompt advice. If you are late, ask about permission for a late appeal; acceptance is not automatic.

Questions parents ask

Does completing an assessment mean the LA must issue an EHCP?

Not automatically. The issue-stage decision turns on whether special educational provision needs to be made in accordance with an EHC plan.

What if the school promises it can provide everything?

Compare that promise with the assessment recommendations, delivery arrangements and available evidence. A general assurance is not the same as a clear account of how identified needs will be met.
