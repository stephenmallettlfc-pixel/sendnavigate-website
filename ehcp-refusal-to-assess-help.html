Help after an EHC needs assessment is refused

The LA has said it will not assess your child. You need to understand why, protect your deadline and decide what to do next—not spend every evening trying to decode the system.

First, check who has said no

A school saying it will not support an application is not the same as the local authority issuing a formal refusal. A parent can request an EHC needs assessment directly without the school's agreement. If you have a written LA decision refusing assessment, there is a right of appeal.

The LA must normally decide whether to assess within six weeks of receiving the request, subject to statutory exceptions. If you are still waiting for a decision, tell the adviser that rather than treating silence as a refusal letter.

The assessment test is “may need”, not “must already prove”

Under section 36(8) of the Children and Families Act 2014, the LA must assess if your child has or may have special educational needs and it may be necessary for special educational provision to be made through an EHC plan. You are asking for an assessment to understand the needs and provision—not proving in advance that a final EHCP must be issued.

A fixed academic gap, a particular number of terms of SEN Support or a school spending threshold cannot replace that legal test. A diagnosis or a privately commissioned report is not a prerequisite for requesting an assessment. Existing evidence still matters: it should describe the needs, their impact and why further assessment may be necessary.

Turn the refusal reason into an evidence question

Useful starting documents include the decision letter, school SEN Support plans, progress and attendance information, professional reports already available and your own specific observations. The task is to connect those documents to the assessment test, rather than produce the largest possible bundle.

Fictional example: a refusal-letter review

This illustrates how to organise the question; it is not advice on an individual case.

LA reason
“Needs can be met through support already available in school.”
Question to examine
Does the evidence nevertheless show that an EHC assessment may be necessary?
Existing evidence
A support plan identifies communication difficulties; review notes show that the difficulties remain despite the agreed strategies.
Information to clarify
What was delivered, what changed, and which needs have not yet been adequately assessed?

Use the refusal-to-assess route

The relevant form is normally SEND35A for a child or young person living in the community. Follow the current GOV.UK instructions for submission, and include the decision letter, mediation certificate and relevant grounds and evidence. Refusal-to-assess appeals are normally decided on written evidence. Do not assume that every case needs a hearing or a representation package.

If assessment is ordered, that does not automatically mean a plan will be issued. If your child has already been assessed and the LA has refused a plan, use the refusal-to-issue help page instead.

Protect the appeal deadline while you decide

For these refusal appeals, the tribunal must normally receive your appeal by the later of two calendar months from the LA decision letter or one month from the mediation certificate. You normally need to contact the mediation adviser within two months, consider mediation and obtain a certificate. You do not have to attend mediation. The decision letter should explain how to contact the relevant adviser.

A conversation with the LA, a school complaint or booking a consultation does not pause the appeal deadline. If the deadline is close, follow the current official submission instructions and seek prompt advice. If you are late, ask about permission for a late appeal; acceptance is not automatic.

Questions parents ask

Can I appeal without the school backing me?

Yes. The school can supply useful evidence, but it does not control your right to request an assessment or appeal a refusal.

Do I need to buy a private assessment first?

No. Start with the evidence already available. Whether a further report would help depends on the issue and any gaps; it is not a universal entry requirement.
