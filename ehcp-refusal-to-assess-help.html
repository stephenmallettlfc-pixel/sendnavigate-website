Help after an EHC needs assessment is refused

A refusal to assess means the local authority has declined an EHC needs assessment; it is not the same as refusing a plan after assessment. School objection or a formal LA refusal?

A school saying it will not support a request is not the same as the local authority (LA) issuing a formal refusal. A parent can request an EHC needs assessment directly without the school's agreement. A written LA decision refusing assessment carries a right of appeal. IPSEA explains assessment requests and the legal test.

The LA must normally decide whether to assess within six weeks of receiving the request, subject to statutory exceptions. If you are still waiting for a decision, describe that separately rather than treating silence as a refusal letter.

The assessment-stage test: “may be necessary”

Under section 36(8) of the Children and Families Act 2014, the LA must assess if your child has or may have special educational needs and it may be necessary for special educational provision to be made through an EHC plan. You are asking for an assessment to understand needs and provision, not proving in advance that a final EHCP must be issued.

A fixed academic gap, a particular number of terms of SEN Support or a school spending threshold cannot replace that legal test. A diagnosis or privately commissioned report is not a prerequisite for requesting an assessment. Existing evidence still matters: it should describe the needs, their impact and why provision through an EHC plan may be necessary.

Refusal letter and assessment evidence

For EHCP refusal to assess appeal help, start with the reason in the decision letter. Useful assessment evidence includes school SEN Support plans, progress and attendance information, professional reports already available and your own specific observations. Connect the evidence to the assessment-stage test rather than collecting the largest possible bundle.

Fictional example: a refusal-letter review

This illustrates how to organise the question; it is not advice on an individual case.

LA reason
“Needs can be met through support already available in school.”
Question to examine
Does the evidence show that provision through an EHC plan may be necessary, so an assessment is required?
Existing evidence
A support plan identifies communication difficulties; review notes show that the difficulties remain despite the agreed strategies.
Information to clarify
What was delivered, what changed, and which needs have not yet been adequately assessed?

For difficulties with current school support, see SEN Support without an EHCP. That concern can inform the evidence, but it is not itself the LA's assessment decision.

Use the refusal-to-assess appeal route

The form for a refusal-to-assess appeal is SEND35A, except where a different custody form applies. The current GOV.UK submission instructions say that refusal-to-assess appeals cannot be submitted through the online appeal service; use the postal or email route. Include the decision letter, mediation certificate and relevant reasons and evidence as instructed. Keep copies and follow the tribunal's directions, including whether there will be a hearing.

If assessment is ordered, that does not automatically mean an EHCP will be issued. If your child has already been assessed and the LA has refused a plan, use the refusal-to-issue help page instead.

Check appeal deadlines while you decide

The tribunal must normally receive your appeal within two calendar months from the date of the LA decision letter or one month from the date of the mediation certificate, whichever is later. See the official deadline and submission guidance.

For a refusal appeal, you normally need to contact a mediation adviser, consider mediation and obtain a certificate. Attending mediation is optional. If you decline it, contact the mediation service for the certificate, as GOV.UK explains. IPSEA's certificate guidance explains the requirement to contact the adviser within two months of the decision letter.

A conversation with the LA, a school complaint or booking a consultation does not pause the appeal deadline. If it is close, follow the official instructions and seek prompt advice. If you are late, ask about permission for a late appeal; acceptance is not automatic.

Questions parents ask

Can I appeal without the school backing me?

Yes. The school can supply useful evidence, but it does not control your right to request an assessment or appeal a refusal.

Do I need to buy a private assessment first?

No. Start with the evidence already available. Whether a further report would help depends on the issue and any gaps; it is not a universal entry requirement.
